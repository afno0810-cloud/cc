The boat

Argus

Our first autonomous surface vessel. A catamaran that finds its way on the water with no one at the wheel, built by students and tested in Trondheim harbour.

Twin hulls

A catamaran design gives Argus a wide and stable platform for sensors and electronics.

3D LiDAR

Scans the area around the boat to find docks, boats and other obstacles.

Stereo camera

Two lenses give Argus depth vision, so it can see and measure what is in front of it.

Dual GNSS antennas

Two satellite antennas tell Argus where it is and which way it is pointing.

Waterproof electronics case

The computer, power and wiring are kept dry inside one sealed case.

Electric thrusters

Thrusters under the hulls push the boat forward and let it turn on the spot.

Njord Challenge · Nyhavna

Four tasks.
No one steering.

At Njord the boats sail a course in the harbour and get one task at a time. The team is not allowed to steer. Scroll to run the course.

  1. A

    Manoeuvring

    Steer through a marked course and show that the boat controls its own movement.

  2. B

    Path finding

    Read the sea marks and find the way through the course without help from the team.

  3. C

    Collision avoidance

    Another vessel crosses the path. The boat has to see it in time and go around it.

  4. D

    Docking

    Find the right spot at the dock and park there on its own.

Course and numbers are a simulation, not logged data from Argus.

Timeline

From Trondheim,
along the coast

  1. 2025

    Marinor is founded

    Four students start Marinor NTNU in Trondheim. The first members build the hulls for Argus in the workshop.

  2. 2026

    First Njord Challenge

    We sail Argus in Njord: The Autonomous Ship Challenge in Trondheim, against university teams from several countries.

  3. 2027

    Win Njord, then RoboBoat

    We want to win the Njord Challenge. After that we want to enter RoboBoat in Florida.

  4. 2028

    A bigger boat

    We plan to start building a much bigger boat.

  5. Later

    The whole coast

    A boat that sails the Norwegian coast on its own, from Kristiansand to Kirkenes, with a crew on board.
